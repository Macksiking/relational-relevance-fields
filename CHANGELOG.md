# Version History

## 3.0 | September 17, 2026

This revision makes the reconstruction of human inner worlds the explicit center of the proposal, rather than treating relevance primarily as an additional weighting of knowledge.

It develops dynamic equilibrium as the proposed background of human appraisal; the recursive relationships among appraisals, their grounds, errors, and corrections; the temporal relationship between experience, action, and consequence; learning from incomplete histories without inventing missing case facts; and human ideals as part of the learning target alongside actual behavior.

The Anna-Paul example now distinguishes who knows what at each point, separates past experience from later reinterpretation, and includes the relationship between self-protection and the person Paul wants to become.

The revision retains the open emergence hypothesis. It does not prescribe a complete psychological ontology or a finished architecture. Proposed experiments separately address reconstruction, latent structure, behavioral effects, evidence handling, and generalization.

Descriptions of related methods have been revised to avoid equating existing alignment training with external filtering alone. Related research is linked explicitly. Possible improvements in alignment, factual reliability, and emergent capabilities are stated as hypotheses rather than demonstrated RRF results.

The release files provide equivalent German and English texts, PDF editions, and linked references.

## 2.0 | Prior discussion draft

Expanded the relational prediction task, the Anna-Paul example, candidate implementations, and a possible experimental program. No publication date is assigned here to this discussion draft.

## 1.0 | May 19, 2026

Initial concept of statistically extracted relational relevance structures and the functional reconstruction of the organizing role of embodied experience. The first version already included the possibility that relevance structures could emerge from relationships.
